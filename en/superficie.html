What? How? Why?

Why?

We inspire people to experience the thrill and the demands of competitive motorsport, whatever their level or location, because we believe the passion for racing should be accessible to everyone.

iRacing exists to democratize access to real racing through the power of simulation. Its purpose is to recreate the motorsport experience with such fidelity and professionalism that amateurs and professionals alike can compete, grow and connect in a fair, challenging and global environment.

What? How? Why?

How?

We recreate motorsport with extreme precision through advanced simulation technology, real sporting rules, an organized global community and a team with experience in professional motorsport.

We organize and run official series in real time using a license system and matchmaking based on skill and safety. We also use real track and telemetry data to replicate how the cars behave.

What? How? Why?

What?

We offer an online racing simulation platform where you can compete in more than 80 official series and thousands of private leagues, with cars, circuits and physics that replicate real motorsport.

We provide a complete platform of official series, private leagues and custom events, accessible with a PC, an internet connection and a compatible controller.

Principles

The communication rules

Go to ValuesGo to Colors

  • Simple

    There must be no information overload, so the user isn’t confused.

  • Understandable

    Information must be clear and understandable for every kind of user. Adults, young people, casual or professional.

  • Layered

    Information must be shown gradually, layer by layer, as the user looks for more specific details on a particular topic.

  • Efficient

    Key tasks (such as registering for a race, looking up results or making a purchase) must be completed in as few steps as possible.

Usability Testing

Context and tasks for the userRead full script

“You’re a new iRacing user. You wanted to get into the world of sim racing, so you subscribed to this simulator. You download it and open the iRacing UI to look for a race. You went through the onboarding, where you were asked which discipline you like to race (Sport Car, Formula Car, Oval, Dirt Oval, Dirt Road). You chose Sport Car and, after a few online Rookie license races, you moved up to the Sport Cars D License.”

Task 1

Try in prototype Task 1 (opens in a new tab)

Register for the D License series “Production Car Challenge by Sim-Lab”, choosing the “Global Mazda MX-5 Cup” as your favorite.

Goals
  • Choose the Mazda car as your favorite.
  • Register for the “Production Car Challenge by Sim-Lab”
  • The task ends when the green banner appears at the top.

Task 2

Try in prototype Task 2 (opens in a new tab)

Register for a race in the D License series “Ferrari Challenge”

Goals
  • Buy the Ferrari Challenge car.
  • The task ends when the snackbar confirming the purchase appears at the top.

Insights

What worked

  • Worked: The execution of task 2 was highly successful, in both speed and understanding.
  • Worked: The confirmation feedback made it very clear when a task was over.
  • Worked: The CTAs were very clear and also helped explain the context of their container.
  • Worked: Despite the space, the complexity and the users’ lack of experience, the tables gave the information needed to understand the context and make decisions.

What didn’t work

  • Didn’t work: Above all, the filters confused users instead of helping them. Either because of their design or because they gave no feedback on what was being filtered.
  • Didn’t work: Choosing a car as a favorite worked, but it was also a bit confusing, since such an important action didn’t have stronger visual hierarchy on screen.
  • Didn’t work: Pre-activating the necessary filters wasn’t the right way to solve the new user problem.

User 1 | Franco

View questionnaire for user 1Watch video of user 1 (opens in a new tab)
  • Problem: He had a hard time finally finding the right series with the filters. He used the search bar, and the preset filters showed him no results.
  • Problem: He didn’t feel he had really chosen the car as a favorite. It should have more visual hierarchy on screen to stand out. Like the cart banner, but for favorites.
  • Worked: He found buying very quick and simple.
  • Suggestion: He’d like to click the licenses to go straight to the races for that discipline and license.

User 2 | Martín

View questionnaire for user 2Watch video of user 2 (opens in a new tab)
  • Problem: He simply couldn’t complete the task of registering for a race. Let alone choosing a car.
  • Problem: The filters weren’t intuitive, either because of their visual design and hierarchy or because he didn’t know filters were being applied to the race list.
  • Worked: He found buying very quick and simple.
  • Suggestion: He’d like the CTAs to say “Race” instead of “Register”
  • Suggestion: He’d identify cars/tracks more easily if they had an image or logo.
  • Suggestion: He’d like the countdown to the race start to be green, to highlight races starting soon
  • Suggestion: Putting SERIES as the page title and in the TABS would help him understand where he is.

User 3 | Fede

View questionnaire for user 3Watch video of user 3 (opens in a new tab)
  • Problem: The dropdown for choosing the car in the cars section wasn’t very intuitive. He mostly ignored it.
  • Problem: Having preselected filters when looking for the race confused him.
  • Worked: He found buying very quick and simple.
  • Suggestion: The menu buttons should include the option to choose the car as a favorite.
  • Suggestion: It should have a more eye-catching icon for adding it as a favorite.

Conclusions

Leaving filters pre-activated, and so poorly designed, was a very bad idea

Filters

The problem that a new user would rather see the few things they can race, instead of everything, is a tricky one.

The solution of leaving the filters activated to allow this view leads to another problem: users struggle to find new races because of the filters.

Causes
  • it’s confusing not to see which filters are being applied.
  • it’s confusing to see the race list for the first time with filters already applied.
  • the filters’ design doesn’t help the user.
